A Boolean search string is a structured query that uses logic operators — AND, OR, and NOT — to narrow or broaden search results. Recruiters have used them for years to find candidates precisely. This tool lets you use the same technique to find jobs and opportunities that standard search filters miss entirely.
Yes — Boolean search works on LinkedIn's free version. Simply paste the generated string into LinkedIn's main search bar and press Enter. You don't need LinkedIn Premium or Recruiter to benefit from this. The Google X-Ray string goes even further by bypassing LinkedIn's search engine entirely and surfacing profiles directly through Google.
LinkedIn's filters are broad and limited — they match on a small set of predefined fields. Boolean search scans the full text of profiles and job listings, including headlines, summaries, and experience sections. This means you find results that LinkedIn's algorithm would never surface through normal filtering. It also gives you full control over the logic rather than relying on LinkedIn's often opaque relevance ranking.
Absolutely. The Google X-Ray tab generates a string specifically formatted for Google, using site:linkedin.com/in/ to search inside LinkedIn's profile pages directly. This is one of the most powerful sourcing techniques used by professional recruiters — it surfaces profiles that LinkedIn's own search hides or deprioritises, especially for free account users.

Google X-Ray is a technique that uses Google to search within a specific website — in this case, LinkedIn. By adding site:linkedin.com/in/ to your search query, you instruct Google to only return results from LinkedIn profile pages. Because Google indexes LinkedIn more deeply than LinkedIn's own search allows free users to access, this method often surfaces profiles that are otherwise invisible. It's free and requires no LinkedIn account at all.
